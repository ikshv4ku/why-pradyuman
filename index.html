Why a chariot

“Harness” is horse tack.

It is the gear that turns a powerful horse into a chariot that goes where you point it. The Kaṭha Upaniṣad drew the same diagram for the mind, and it reads like an agent architecture.

The metaphor is his own: he played the lead in a classical Sanskrit play and volunteers with the Sanskrit Club.

आत्मानं रथिनं विद्धि शरीरं रथमेव तु ।बुद्धिं तु सारथिं विद्धि मनः प्रग्रहमेव च ॥

ātmānaṁ rathinaṁ viddhi śarīraṁ ratham eva tubuddhiṁ tu sārathiṁ viddhi manaḥ pragraham eva ca

Know the self as the rider, and the body as the chariot. Know the intellect as the charioteer, and the mind as the reins.

Kaṭha Upaniṣad 1.3.3

indriyāṇi hayān āhur viṣayāṁs teṣu gocarān

The senses, they say, are the horses, and the things they reach for are the roads they run on.

The ride

Five stations, one chariot.

Scroll to drive. At each station: one part of the harness, and what he has already built for it.

Station 1 of 5

Ashva अश्व

The horses are the models

Pick the horse by cost per solved task, not price per token.

At Databricks he wrote an efficiency-vs-cost report on every Claude model, every OpenAI model then on the market, Grok and Cursor’s agent. His whole team read it, and it went out on the relevant org-wide Slack channel to everyone at Databricks.

Station 2 of 5

Rashmi रश्मि

The reins are context and memory

Filesystem as memory: agents open only the branch a task needs.

All his agents and subagents read and wrote one hierarchical tree of markdown files, so a task opens only the branch it needs (progressive disclosure). On Databricks’ company-wide AI usage dashboard, his usage ran 2–3× below the intern cohort. That is a peer comparison, not a controlled test.

Station 3 of 5 · at Databricks

Sārathi सारथि

The charioteer is orchestration

He drives a production harness, and builds agents his team adopted.

At Databricks he did his engineering through Isaac, the internal harness Databricks’ CTO has publicly described as a wrapper on Claude Code and Codex, and worked on its skills and context management. Subagents, MCP servers and skills were his daily toolkit.

How his setup spread

  1. His personal setup
  2. Adopted by many on his 7-person team
  3. Pitched for org-wide adoption in his final intern presentation
  4. Demo acknowledged up to Senior Director and VP level

His team’s feedback was rich, and it shaped each iteration.

Value-maxxing. He helped shift AI usage from token-maxxing to value-maxxing by tiering users on tokens used against output delivered. Measure cost per outcome, not tokens burned.

Station 3 of 5 · his daily loop

He ran his internship as one agent loop.

The journal wrote up each day’s work on a set of Confluence pages, leaving detailed daily, weekly and monthly documentation: memory consolidation from episodes to summaries. The critic compared his work with the team’s, named his mistakes and limitations, and gave him strict pointers: a reflection loop pointed at the engineer, not the code.

Station 3 of 5 · in public

And a multi-agent system that scored in public.

Station 4 of 5

Ratha रथ

The chariot is the harness he designs

SMRITI: one trustworthy memory for a whole team of agents.

Grown from his team-KB prototype at Databricks, which a few teammates used. Tap a stage.

Write path · once per changeShred and scrub. Each note becomes atomic knowledge cards, and secrets are removed before anything leaves the laptop.

Shruti in, Smriti out.What one engineer heard becomes what the team remembers.

A design, not a benchmark: any savings are estimates. It stands on ACE, Graphiti and MemCurrent.

Station 5 of 5

Mārga मार्ग

The road is the Meet, 11–14 Dec, IIT Guwahati

Measure a bare baseline early, then win on the delta.

The sentence to earn: “Same model, our harness: X% to Y% at Z% of the cost per solved task.” LangChain moved one model from 52.8% to 66.5% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 this way.

0:00a 12-hour build, scaled to the real clock12:00
  1. 0:00Read and define. One-line metric; 10–20 auto-graded evals
  2. 0:45Baseline. Bare loop, JSONL traces, three runs
  3. 1:45Tools. Absolute paths, terse errors, spill to files
  4. 3:30Verification. Checks tool, completion gate, loop detector
  5. 5:00Context. Short AGENTS.md, compaction at ~60%
  6. 6:30Safety. Workspace-only writes, step and dollar budgets
  7. 7:15Trace-driven fixes. One fix per failure mode
  8. 9:30Ablations. Remove one part at a time
  9. 10:30Demo. Results table, live trace, recorded fallback

Under the hood

A harness is a systems program with an LLM in the loop.

He has built, or co-built, most of the layers it rests on. When an agent breaks, he knows which layer to open.

He builtWhat it wasThe harness problem it prepares him for

  • Veloxa C compiler from scratch

    What it wasLexer, recursive-descent parser, IR, x86-64 codegen, GoogleTest suite

    Prepares him forTool calls are a grammar. Parsers and validators are how agents stay honest; tests are back-pressure.

  • Keypera Raft key-value store

    What it wasLeader election, majority replication, CRC32 sharding over 3 clusters and 9 nodes, zero-downtime shard migration

    Prepares him forMany agents writing one memory is a consistency problem.

  • File servermulti-threadedTeam project, 4 people

    What it was100 concurrent clients, per-file reader-writer locks, salted SHA-256 auth, role-based access

    Prepares him forParallel subagents on shared files; permissions and sandboxing.

  • Request-path observabilityDatabricks, Network Foundations

    What it wasFirst-failing-hop attribution, an end-to-end correlation ID, log sampling consistent across services that never drops a failure

    Prepares him forTracing and debugging agent runs: an agent run is the same kind of multi-hop chain.

    What it wasDual-issue, 5-stage pipeline on a custom 16-bit ISA

    Prepares him forThroughput thinking: what can run in parallel, and what must wait.

What he’d build next

Three ideas he would bring, each grown from work he has done.

These are proposals, not shipped products. Each one extends something on this page.

Idea, not shipped

A flight recorder for agents

His request-path observability, applied to agent runs. Every tool call gets a correlation ID, the failing step is attributed automatically, and sampling keeps every failed trajectory.

Grows fromRequest-path observability at Databricks: failure-phase attribution, correlation IDs, consistent sampling that never drops a failure.

Idea, not shipped

A value router

Send each task to the cheapest model whose expected success clears the bar, and judge it on cost per solved task.

Grows fromHis efficiency-vs-cost model report and his value-maxxing tiers.

Idea, not shipped

SMRITI for the whole org

His team-memory design, scaled from one team to many. More authors means more to merge and reconcile, which is what SMRITI’s middle stages are for.

Grows fromSMRITI, and the team knowledge-base prototype a few teammates used.
